What the design gust means

The design gust is the strongest 3-second gust, expected once in 50 years at the crest, that the turbine is built to survive. A site counts as within the limit when its gust is at or below the slider. The IEC marks are the class reference speeds multiplied by 1.4: class III 37.5 m/s (52.5 m/s gust), class II 42.5 m/s (59.5) and class I 50 m/s (70). Snowy sites, with at least 100 mm of snow water a year, have their gust multiplied by the snow factor to allow for ice and snow loads.

How to use the page

  1. Choose new ridges or existing wind farms.
  2. Move the design gust, or pick an IEC class.
  3. Read the energy, sites and rotors it unlocks in the panel and the chart.

About these numbers. Gust values use the national wind code where we have assessed it. Elsewhere they are estimates from ERA5 yearly maxima (2015–2024), scaled to code values; for existing wind farms the estimate is calibrated against the wind farms already standing. Protected sites are those inside Natura 2000 or an OpenStreetMap national park or nature reserve. Energy is the modelled gross WindNext potential per site, a technical maximum before wake, availability and electrical losses, with an uncertainty of roughly ±25–30%. It is not a build plan or a commitment.
